## Trouble in Studyland

The College's Year 12 community is in shock tonight after a senior member of staff burst into the Study Hall in the Library and confiscated the phones of at least 20 students who were studying peacefully.

The incident comes as the College Board has increased its rhetoric against mobile devices, with leading experts saying an event like this was an inevitable outcome.

The Study Hall had been a long-lived sanctuary zone for Year 12s wanting to use their phones, with mobile phone policy enforcement for senior students already being minimal across the School.

This was until 11:49 am AEDT (2:34 pm for Chatham Island readers), when a senior member of staff exchanged words with students. The students were reported to have apologised to the staff member, but it seemed that wasn't enough.

One victim recounts the following:

> "He looked at me really angrily. His face turned red and I was concerned it might explode, or that he may at least turn into a tomato. Then, he grabbed it off me and said that it was strictly against school policy."

Another victim reported almost losing their Duolingo streak due to the phone confiscations.

A students' rights group quickly condemned the actions of the senior staff member, calling for the rights of students to be respected, a repeal of the mobile phone ban for senior students who are studying, and a toning down of aggressive anti-phone rhetoric.

The College responded, writing that such events occurred after many months of warnings, and that the Board must engage in such actions to maintain the integrity of its sovereignty and uphold the rule of law in the College.

The untold damage that events like this do to their victims is difficult to articulate, with those who had their phones snatched today only beginning to come to terms with the long road to recovery ahead.
